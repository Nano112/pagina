# On writing it down

This blog exists because a commit message got to four hundred words and I could not link to it.

The reasoning behind a change has to live somewhere, and the commit is the obvious place: it is
attached to the diff, it is versioned with the code, and nobody has to go looking for it. That
works right up until a second person needs to read it, or until the same reasoning applies to a
change in a different repository, or until six months pass and the only way to find it is to know
which commit it was.

A post has a URL. That is nearly the whole of the argument.

The rest of it is that a commit message is written under time pressure, at the end of the work,
when the thinking is done and you want to be finished. A post is written afterwards, which is
usually when you find out that the second half of the reasoning was wrong.

I do not expect to keep this up. Most blogs stop. But the nightly export request in
[a week of reading logs](a-week-of-reading-logs.md) had been running since February before anyone
looked at it, and I would rather have written that down somewhere findable than not.
